## Visa Options for Digital Nomads in Indonesia

### Tourist Visa (Visa on Arrival – VOA)

The Tourist Visa allows stays up to **30 days**, with possible extension for an additional month. However, it prohibits income-generating activities within Indonesia.

### B211A Social, Cultural, or Business Visa

This visa suits those staying between 30 days and six months for social, cultural, or business purposes. It's renewable every 60 days but doesn't permit work for local companies or Indonesian salary income.

### Digital Nomad Visa

Indonesia introduced a Digital Nomad Visa in 2025 enabling remote workers to legally work for foreign companies while residing in the country for extended periods, streamlining the legal framework for foreign remote workers.

### KITAS (Temporary Stay Permit)

The KITAS accommodates longer stays and typically requires a sponsor—either an Indonesian citizen or local company. It permits freelance work and payments from foreign sources.

## Work Permits and Taxation

Digital nomads must secure appropriate documentation before engaging in income-generating activities. Standard options include work permits (IMTA) for Indonesian company employment and temporary work permits for short-term projects.

**Taxation matters:** Remote workers for foreign companies generally avoid Indonesian income tax unless staying over **183 days annually**. Extended stays trigger tax residency status, subjecting worldwide income to local taxation.

## Compliance Requirements

To maintain legal status:

- Renew visas before expiration
- Maintain health insurance and financial documentation
- Avoid illegal employment with Indonesian entities
- Never accept payments from Indonesian sources on tourist visas

## Benefits of Indonesia

Advantages include affordable living costs, tropical climate, established digital nomad communities (particularly in Bali), and rich cultural experiences.
